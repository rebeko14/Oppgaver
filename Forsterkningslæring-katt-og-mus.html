🎲 Explore (random moves) 🎯 Exploit (learned policy)
What does this do? Controls how often the cat tries a random move vs. uses what it has already learned. A high value (close to 1) means the cat explores freely — great early on when it knows nothing. A low value (close to 0) means the cat always picks the move it thinks is best — useful once it has learned enough. 💡 Try setting it high (0.9) then low (0.1) and compare how learning speed changes.
What does this do? How strongly each new experience overwrites old knowledge. A high α (e.g. 0.9) means the agent learns fast but can be unstable — one bad move changes everything. A low α (e.g. 0.1) means slow, steady learning that is less sensitive to single events. 💡 Think of it like how quickly you update your opinion after one bad experience.
What does this do? How much the agent values future rewards compared to immediate ones. A high γ (e.g. 0.99) means the cat is patient — it plans many steps ahead to reach the mouse. A low γ (e.g. 0.2) means the cat is short-sighted — it only cares about the very next step. 💡 Imagine the difference between saving for retirement vs. only thinking about today's lunch.

Key Concepts

Explore: Random moves → discovers new paths
Exploit: Use learned Q-values → follow best known path
Q-Value: Expected future reward for (state, action)
Bellman Eq: Q(s,a) ← Q + α[r + γ·max Q(s') − Q]
